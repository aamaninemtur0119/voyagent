Source page: "Visitor Visa," U.S. Department of State, travel.state.gov (Tourism & Visit section).

**Who needs it**: Generally, a citizen of a foreign country who wishes to travel to the United States must first obtain a visa — either a nonimmigrant visa for a temporary stay or an immigrant visa for permanent residence. Visitor visas are nonimmigrant visas for people who want to enter the U.S. temporarily for business (B-1 visa), tourism (B-2 visa), or a combination of both (B-1/B-2 visa). Citizens of Visa Waiver Program (VWP) countries and citizens of Canada and Bermuda are generally exempt from this visa requirement for short visits (see separate files for those categories).

**Permitted activities**:
- B-1 (business): consult with business associates, attend a scientific/educational/professional/business convention or conference, settle an estate, negotiate a contract.
- B-2 (tourism): tourism/vacation, visiting friends or relatives, medical treatment, participation in social events hosted by fraternal/social/service organizations, amateur participation in musical/sports/similar events without pay, enrollment in a short recreational (non-credit) course of study.
- NOT permitted on a visitor visa: study, employment, paid performances, arrival as a ship/aircraft crewmember, work as foreign press/media, permanent residence, or birth tourism.

**Application process**:
1. Complete the online Nonimmigrant Visa Application, Form DS-160, and print the confirmation page.
2. Upload a photo meeting the Photograph Requirements as part of DS-160.
3. Schedule a visa interview, generally at the U.S. Embassy/Consulate in the applicant's country of residence. Wait times vary by location, season, and visa category.
4. Pay the non-refundable visa application fee: **$185** (stated fee on the page). A visa issuance fee may also apply depending on nationality ("Select your nationality to see Issuance Fee").
5. Gather required documents: passport valid for travel to the U.S. (must be valid at least 6 months beyond the intended period of stay, unless exempted by country-specific agreement), DS-160 confirmation page, fee payment receipt, photo. Additional documents may be requested to establish purpose of trip, intent to depart after the trip, and ability to pay for the trip (e.g., evidence of employment/family ties).
6. Attend the visa interview: a consular officer determines qualification; ink-free digital fingerprint scans are usually taken during the interview.

**Interview requirement**: Interviews are generally required for visa applicants, with certain limited exceptions; consular officers may require an interview of any applicant regardless of exception eligibility. Some applicants renewing a visa in certain classes may qualify for the Interview Waiver (IW) program, allowing renewal without an in-person interview — availability depends on the specific embassy/consulate.

**Validity/notes**:
- A visa allows a foreign citizen to travel to a U.S. port of entry and request permission to enter; a visa does not guarantee entry — CBP officers at the port of entry decide admission.
- A valid U.S. visa in an expired passport remains valid; it can be used together with a new valid passport.
- B1/B2 visa holders are not permitted to accept employment or work in the United States.
- Failure to depart on time results in "out of status," and under INA Section 222(g), such a visa is automatically voided for future entries.
- No guaranteed issuance — applicants should not make final travel plans or buy tickets before visa issuance.

**Nationality-specific notes stated on this page**:
- Citizens of China with 10-year B1/B2/B1-B2 visas must update biographical/application information every two years (or upon new passport/visa) via the Electronic Visa Update System (EVUS, www.EVUS.gov). As of September 30, 2025, a $30.00 fee is charged for each new EVUS enrollment.
- Citizens/permanent residents of Mexico generally must have a nonimmigrant visa or Border Crossing Card ("Laser Visa"); the B-1/B-2 and Border Crossing Card have been combined into one document (DSP-150).
- Citizens of Canada and Bermuda generally do NOT require visas for visit, tourism, and temporary business travel (see us-entry-canada.md for details).
