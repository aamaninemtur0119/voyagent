**CONFIRMED FINDING: There is no unified/harmonized EU-wide study visa process. Study (and all other) long-stay visas are national competence, issued under each member state's own national procedures — not the common Schengen visa framework.**

**Exact quote establishing this** (from the EU visa policy page): "What is EU visa policy? Under this key policy, all 29 countries in the Schengen area apply the same visa rules. Non-EU nationals need a visa to enter the Schengen area for a visit of up to 90 days in any 180-day period... **Visas for stays exceeding 90 days are subject to national procedures.**" The same page also states: "The EU visa code sets out the procedures and conditions for issuing **short-stay** visas" — explicitly scoping the harmonized "Visa Code" (Regulation (EC) No 810/2009, also cited in schengen-airport-transit-visa.md) to short stays only, not to long-stay national visas such as study permits.

**This is analogous to, and reinforces, the existing corpus finding in schengen-airport-transit-visa.md**, which established that even within the *harmonized* short-stay framework, airport transit visa nationality lists combine an EU-wide common list with individually-set member-state additions. Here the situation is more categorical: study visas (almost always required for stays over 90 days, since most study programs run longer than a 90-day tourist stay) fall entirely outside the common Schengen short-stay system and are governed purely by the destination country's own national immigration law.

**Practical confirmation — the EU Immigration Portal's own structure**: The EU Immigration Portal (linked from the same Commission visa-policy page) organizes its study-related guidance as a large set of separate per-country pages — e.g., "Student in Poland," "Student in Slovakia," "Student in Slovenia," "Student in Spain," "Student in Sweden," and so on for every EU member state individually — rather than a single unified "Student in the EU/Schengen area" page. This structure itself demonstrates the national-competence finding in practice.

**Example of a national process (Poland, fetched as one illustrative country page — NOT to be generalized to other member states)**: Poland's national ('D' symbol, '09' annotation) long-stay study visa: valid up to 1 year, an 80 euro fee, and a 30-day decision timeline, as an alternative/precursor to a Temporary Residence Permit (first permit granted for 15 months or "academic year plus 3 months" if shorter; 340 PLN stamp duty + 100 PLN card fee; ~60-day processing; requires enrollment certificate, health insurance, and proof of at least 776 PLN/month in funds). **These Poland-specific figures do not apply to any other Schengen/EU country** — that is precisely the point of this file's finding.

**What this means for the agent**: There is no single "Schengen study visa" rule this corpus can state generally, the way it can for the harmonized 90/180 short-stay rule (schengen-90-180-rule.md) or the ETIAS/short-stay visa-requirement lists. **For any traveler asking about a Schengen-area study visa, the agent should ask the traveler which specific member state they intend to study in**, then consult that country's national immigration authority — the same posture the corpus already takes for airport transit visa lists being "set per member state" (schengen-airport-transit-visa.md) and for the missing/unresolved individual national-list specifics in that file. This file should be treated as the authoritative "this is genuinely not a unified EU rule, don't fabricate one" reference for study-purpose Schengen queries, not as a source of any specific country's study-visa requirements.

**Gap/caveat**: This session did not fetch national study-visa pages for all (or even most) Schengen member states — only Poland's, as a single illustrative example already surfaced by the EU Immigration Portal search. If a specific member state's study-visa process is needed for a real query, a fresh fetch of that country's national immigration-ministry page (or its EU Immigration Portal country page, itself sourced from national authorities) is required before any country-specific figures can be presented as confirmed.
