Source page: "Transit Visa," Consulate-General of Japan in Miami, page dated 2025/9/10 (as listed on the page itself).

**Who needs it**: "Transit visas are issued to foreign nationals (whose country of citizenship is NOT on the visa exemption page) traveling through Japan en route to another country and staying for **1 night (or less) only**." (See japan-visa-exemption-list.md for which nationalities are visa-exempt in the first place — those nationalities do not need a transit visa either, for stays within their exemption period.)

**Who does NOT need one**: "Travelers with immediate connection flights which only requires change of an aircraft within the same airport, and are not required to go through immigration clearance do not require a visa."

**Duration limit / when a different visa is required instead**: The transit visa covers a stay of 1 night or less. "Note: anyone staying for 2 or more nights must apply for a Temporary Visitor visa" instead (see japan-business-visa.md, which documents that Japan's Temporary Visitor / Short-Term Stay visa is the general short-stay category covering tourism, business, and visiting relatives, capped at 90 days).

**Permitted activities**: "Activities for this type of visa are limited to sightseeing and rest" — i.e., a transit visa does not authorize business meetings, work, or other activities beyond passing through and resting during the layover.

**Required documents** (as listed by the Miami consulate; document requirements can vary slightly by the consulate with jurisdiction over the applicant, per the note on other MOFA-network pages in this corpus):
1. Valid passport, properly signed, with available visa pages.
2. One application form, completely filled out and signed (nationals of Russia and CIS countries submit two, each with a photo attached).
3. One photograph (2 in. x 1.4 in., white background, taken within 6 months).
4. Copy of round-trip flight itinerary (reservation, not necessarily a purchased ticket, covering the applicant's name, date, and flight information).
5. Copy of hotel confirmation for every day of the stay in Japan.
6. Copy of the visa for the onward country/countries being visited, if a visa is required there (or proof of visa exemption for that country).
7. Proof of sufficient funds (applicant's or financial guarantor's latest 3 months' bank/savings statements) to cover all travel expenses.
8. Proof of legal immigration status in the country of application (this consulate's checklist is U.S.-specific — e.g., a Green Card or valid U.S. visa/I-94; F or J visa holders must also submit endorsed I-20/DS-2019 copies) — this item will differ for applicants applying elsewhere.
9. Proof of residential address within the consulate's jurisdiction (this item is consulate-specific and would differ elsewhere).
10. Payment of the visa fee, if the applicant's country of citizenship is not fee-exempt (see japan-visa-fees.md, which documents MOFA's general fee schedule: "700 yen for a transit visa" as of a snapshot dated shortly before a July 1, 2026 fee revision — that file's confidence is `uncertain` for the post-revision amount, and the same caveat applies here; the current transit-visa fee should be re-verified before being presented as settled).
11. An Authorization Form, if someone other than the applicant submits/picks up the documents (required for applicants 15+).

**Gap/caveat**: This session sourced the transit-visa description from a single U.S. consulate's page (Miami) because the general mofa.go.jp transit-visa page and other consulates' transit pages (e.g., Atlanta) either returned HTTP 403 directly or the Wayback Machine snapshot was temporarily unavailable during this session. The core rule (1 night/less; visa exemption interacts with it; 2+ nights requires Temporary Visitor visa instead; no immigration-clearance-free connections are exempt) is stated in general terms on the page and is not itself U.S.-jurisdiction-specific, but item-level document requirements (proof of U.S. legal status, Florida residency, etc.) are specific to applicants residing in that consulate's jurisdiction and should not be treated as universal — applicants elsewhere should confirm the equivalent requirements with the Japanese embassy/consulate covering their own place of residence, per the general application-jurisdiction rule confirmed on mofa.go.jp's visa FAQ page (fetched separately this session): "You can apply for the visa at the Japanese Embassy / Consulate General in your own country (region) or country (region) of residence."

**Note on a possible July 2026 transit-visa-category change**: A web search during this session surfaced a claim, from a non-official source, that "from July 1, 2026, the Transit Visa category will be merged into the Single or Double-entry category." This was **not confirmed against an official mofa.go.jp or emb-japan.go.jp page** in this session (the Miami page fetched above, dated 2025/9/10, still describes Transit Visa as a distinct category) and should not be presented to a user as settled fact without further verification against a current official source.
