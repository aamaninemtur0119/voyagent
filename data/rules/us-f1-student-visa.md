Source page: "Student Visa," U.S. Department of State, travel.state.gov (Study & Exchange section).

**F vs. M visa**: "You must have a student visa to travel to the United States to study. Your course of study and the type of school you plan to attend determine whether you need an F visa or an M visa." F visa: university/college, high school, private elementary school, seminary, conservatory, another academic institution (including language training programs). M visa: vocational or other recognized nonacademic institution (other than a language training program).

**Cannot study on a visitor visa or the Visa Waiver Program**: "You must have a student visa (F or M visa) to travel to the United States to study. You may not study after entering on a visitor (B) visa," unless eligible for and granted a change of status by USCIS, or through the VWP — except for recreational (non-credit) study as part of a tourist visit, which a B visa permits. Study leading to a U.S.-conferred degree or certificate is never permitted on a visitor (B) visa, "even if it is for a short duration."

**Step 1 — SEVP-approved school and SEVIS**: The first step is applying to a school approved by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP). If accepted, the student is registered in the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System (SEVIS) and must pay the **SEVIS I-901 fee** (dollar amount not stated on this page — see gap note below). The school issues Form I-20, which must be presented to the consular officer at the visa interview. Spouse/children who will live with the student in the U.S. must also enroll in SEVIS and get individual Form I-20s (but do not pay the SEVIS fee).

**Application process** (same DS-160-based process as other nonimmigrant visas):
1. Complete the online Nonimmigrant Visa Application, Form DS-160, print the confirmation page.
2. Upload a photo meeting the Photograph Requirements as part of the DS-160.
3. Schedule a visa interview, generally at the U.S. Embassy/Consulate where the applicant lives. Interviews are generally required with certain limited exceptions.
4. **Timing — new vs. continuing students**:
   - New students: F and M visas "can be issued up to 365 days before the start date of a course of study." However, entry to the U.S. is not permitted "more than 30 days before the start date."
   - Continuing students: visas "may be issued at any time," as long as the student is currently enrolled at a SEVP-approved school and in SEVIS; continuing students may enter the U.S. at any time before classes start.
5. Pay the non-refundable visa application fee: **$185** (stated on the page). A visa issuance fee may also apply depending on nationality.
6. Gather required documents: passport valid at least 6 months beyond intended period of stay (unless exempted), DS-160 confirmation page, fee payment receipt, photo, and **Form I-20** ("Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant (F-1) Student Status," or the M-1 equivalent) — issued by the school once SEVIS registration is complete, signed by both the student and school official.
7. Additional documentation may be requested: evidence of academic preparation (transcripts, diplomas, standardized test scores), intent to depart the U.S. after completing the course of study, and how educational/living/travel costs will be paid.
8. Attend the interview; ink-free digital fingerprint scans are usually taken during the interview. Administrative processing may follow.

**Entering the United States**: A visa does not guarantee entry — CBP officials at the port of entry decide admission after the traveler presents passport, visa, and Form I-20; an admission stamp or paper Form I-94 is then issued.

**Extending / departing on time**: "Foreign students in the United States with F visas must depart the United States within 60 days after the program end date listed on Form I-20, including any authorized practical training." Extensions can be requested through USCIS. Failure to depart on time results in being "out of status," and under INA Section 222(g) the visa is automatically voided for future entries; it may also cause future visa ineligibility.

**Change of status**: If circumstances change while in the U.S. (e.g., marriage to a U.S. citizen, a job offer), a change of nonimmigrant status may be requested through USCIS; this does not itself require a new visa, but departing the U.S. afterward requires applying for a new visa in the appropriate category.

**Optional Practical Training (OPT)**: Students authorized for OPT must have Form I-20 endorsed for OPT and apply to USCIS for an Employment Authorization Document (EAD). OPT is temporary employment directly related to the F-1 student's field of study.

**Spouse and children**: Spouse and unmarried minor children who intend to reside with the student may apply for F-2 or M-2 visas; the school must issue them individual Form I-20s (no SEVIS fee for them). Minor children may attend school in the U.S. while accompanying the student.

**Public secondary school restriction**: There are restrictions on F-1 students attending U.S. public schools (see the page's linked "Foreign Students in Public Schools" sub-page — not independently fetched in this session).

**Re-entry after temporary absence**: A valid U.S. visa in an expired passport remains valid and usable with a new valid passport.

**Citizens of Canada and Bermuda**: "generally do not require visas to enter the United States as students," although a valid Form I-20 must be presented at admission.

**Gap/caveat**: This page states that the SEVIS I-901 fee must be paid but does not state the dollar amount (it links out to the ICE/SEVP website, which is outside the travel.state.gov / official-domain scope of this research session, so the figure is not included here and should not be assumed). Likewise, no minimum-course-length threshold for F-1 vs. other short-term study options is stated on this page.
