Source: "Visitor Visa" and "Travel Without a Visa" pages, U.S. Department of State, travel.state.gov.

**No visa is required for Canadian citizens** for short visit, tourism, and temporary business travel purposes. Exact text from the official page: "Citizens of Canada and Bermuda generally do not require visas to enter the United States, for visit, tourism and temporary business travel purposes."

**Important distinction — this is NOT the Visa Waiver Program (VWP) / ESTA mechanism.** The travel.state.gov site structures "Travel Without a Visa" into two separate, distinct pathways:
1. Visa Waiver Program (VWP) — for citizens of specific participating countries, requiring ESTA authorization, limited to visits of 90 days or less.
2. "Citizens of Canada and Bermuda" — listed as its own separate category, distinct from the VWP. The page states this exemption is a general non-visa-requirement rooted in Canadian/Bermudian citizens' longstanding treatment under U.S. immigration law, not a VWP/ESTA enrollment.

The fetched pages did not state that Canadians need to obtain ESTA or any other pre-travel electronic authorization; no ESTA requirement for Canadian citizens is mentioned anywhere on these two official pages. The mechanism for Canadian citizens is simply presenting valid passport/proof of citizenship at the U.S. port of entry, where a CBP officer determines admission (consistent with general U.S. entry rules described on the Visitor Visa page).

Additional resources referenced (not fetched in this session): U.S. Embassy Ottawa website, U.S. Consulate Hamilton website, and the CBP website — cited as sources for further Canadian-specific travel information.

**Scope caveat**: These pages describe visa-exemption generally for "visit, tourism and temporary business travel purposes." They do not itemize every possible travel purpose; the visitor.html page notes that "some purposes of travel may require a visa" even for Canadians/Bermudians is not explicitly stated in the fetched text — the source only confirms the general exemption for visit/tourism/temporary business. For purposes outside these categories, this data does not provide a definitive answer and should not be assumed.
