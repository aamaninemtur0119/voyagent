Cross-referenced against the official Short-stay Calculator page (https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen/border-crossing/short-stay-calculator_en), listed on the same domain.

**The 90/180-day rule**: Non-EU nationals (whether visa-exempt or Schengen-visa holders for short stays) may stay in the Schengen area for a maximum of **90 days within any 180-day period**. The 180-day window is a rolling window: for any given day of stay, the traveler must count back 180 days and ensure the total number of days spent in the Schengen area during that window does not exceed 90. Travelers may enter and exit multiple times, but cumulative days in the area within any rolling 180-day window may not exceed 90.

**What is required for a stay EXCEEDING 90 days**: "Visas for stays exceeding 90 days are subject to national procedures" — meaning long-stay requirements are NOT harmonized EU-wide but are handled individually by each Schengen member state. Specifically:
- To stay in a given Schengen country for more than 90 days, a traveler needs either a **long-stay (national/type D) visa** or a **residence permit** issued by that specific member state, obtained through that country's own national procedures.
- To move from one EU/Schengen country to another for a stay of more than 90 days, the traveler needs a long-stay visa or residence permit specific to the country they are moving to.
- Periods of stay authorized under a residence permit or a long-stay visa are NOT counted toward the 90/180-day short-stay calculation — i.e., time spent under a long-stay visa/residence permit sits outside the short-stay 90/180 budget.

**Category takeover**: In short, exceeding 90 days within a 180-day window requires transitioning out of the short-stay (type C / visa-exempt) framework entirely and into a national long-stay visa (type D) or residence permit issued by the specific member state where the extended stay occurs — this is a national-level process, not a Schengen/EU-wide one.
