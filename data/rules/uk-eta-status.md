**Current status (as of the pages fetched on 2026-08-23): the ETA requirement IS live and enforced, not just announced.**

Timeline established by the official pages/announcements:
- 27 November 2024 (gov.uk news): "From today all eligible non-European visitors can apply for an ETA and will need one to travel to the UK from 8 January 2025." This wave covered non-European, non-visa-national visitors (explicitly named in that release: USA, Canada, Australia, Gulf Cooperation Council nationals).
- Europeans (non-UK/Irish, visa-exempt) became subject to the requirement from 2 April 2025 (applications opened 5 March 2025), per the same rollout announcement.
- 24 November 2025 (gov.uk news, "No permission, no travel: UK set to enforce ETA scheme"): announced that starting **25 February 2026**, carrier-level enforcement begins — "Visitors from 85 nationalities... will not be able to legally travel to the UK without an ETA from 25 February 2026." Named the United States, Canada, and France as examples of the 85 nationalities.
- April 2026 Home Office factsheet (published after the 25 February 2026 enforcement date, so after today's 2026-08-23 cutoff it is the most recent status source found): confirms the scheme is "actively enforced" — "Visitors without an ETA will not be able to board their transport and cannot travel to the UK, unless exempt."

**Canada — CONFIRMED required.** The live https://www.gov.uk/eta and https://www.gov.uk/guidance/electronic-travel-authorisation-eta pages both explicitly state: "You usually need an ETA rather than a visa if you're from Europe, the USA, Australia, Canada or certain other countries." Canada is named by name on the current live official page. Canada was also explicitly named in both the 2024 rollout announcement and the 2025 enforcement announcement as a nationality subject to the requirement.

**Brazil — NOT independently confirmed by name on any official page fetched in this session.** None of the live GOV.UK pages fetched (gov.uk/eta, gov.uk/guidance/electronic-travel-authorisation-eta, gov.uk/eta/when-not-need-eta, dual-citizens guide) name Brazil explicitly — they refer only to "Europe, the USA, Australia, Canada or certain other countries" without an exhaustive list, and direct users to an interactive eligibility checker tool that could not be queried via automated fetch. The 27 November 2024 gov.uk announcement covering the January 2025 mandatory date used the category "all eligible non-European visitors" without naming Brazil specifically; Brazil is a UK visa-exempt nationality for short stays and so would fall within that category by inference, but this file does not have a direct official-page citation naming Brazil.

**Cost/validity as stated on current live pages**: £20 per ETA (note: this differs from the £10 figure quoted in the original 2023-2024 launch announcements — the fee appears to have increased at some point; the £20 figure is what the live gov.uk/eta and gov.uk/guidance/electronic-travel-authorisation-eta pages state as of this fetch). Valid for multiple journeys, stays of up to 6 months each, for up to 2 years or until the passport expires, whichever is sooner.

**Who is exempt from needing an ETA** (per gov.uk/eta/when-not-need-eta): British or Irish citizens (including dual citizens, who use their British/Irish passport or right-of-abode documentation instead — they cannot obtain an ETA), visa holders, those with UK settled/pre-settled status or right of abode, Jersey/Guernsey/Isle of Man residents with permission, airside transit passengers not passing through border control, British Overseas Territories passport holders, British National (Overseas) passport holders, Ireland residents travelling from Ireland/Guernsey/Jersey/Isle of Man, French school trip participants (specific scheme), and those with other immigration control exemptions.
