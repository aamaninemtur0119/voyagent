**Finding: Japan does NOT have a legally distinct "business visa." Business travel is one of several purposes covered under the same Temporary Visitor / Short-Term Stay visa (the same visa category used for tourism), per the Consulate-General of Japan in Boston's official page "Visiting Japan (Visa for Short-Term Stay)," page dated February 24, 2026, filed under the site's "Temporary Visitor Visa" section.**

**Who can use a Visa for Short-Term Stay** (exact text from the page): "For those who intend to stay in Japan for 90 days or less for such purposes as follows. (This category excludes profit-making operations and paid activities.)" The listed purposes include, side by side:
- sightseeing; recreation; sports
- visiting relatives, friends, or acquaintances; visiting a sick person; attending a wedding or funeral ceremony
- participating in athletic tournaments/contests as an amateur
- **"business purposes (such as market research, business liaison, business consultation, signing a contract, or providing after-sale service for imported machinery)"**
- inspecting or visiting plants, trade fairs, etc.
- attending lectures, explanatory meetings, academic surveys, or research presentations
- religious pilgrimages or visits; friendship visits to sister cities/schools

The page states plainly that persons traveling "for a different purpose, such as students, workers, journalists, entertainers etc., must apply for a different visa in the appropriate category" — but tourism and business are explicitly grouped together under this one Short-Term Stay visa, not split into separate visa types.

**Document checklist confirms this**: The consulate's required-document table lists four purpose columns on a single application form — "Business," "Conference," "For Pleasure," and "Visiting friends, etc." — all under the one "Visa for Short-Term Stay." Core documents (application form, passport, photo, proof of legal status, flight reservation, proof of residence, proof of funds) are required for all four purposes; a handful of purpose-specific documents differ (e.g., business applicants need a letter from the home institution/business firm and, in most cases, an invitation letter from the host Japanese institution/business firm; conference applicants need a conference brochure or organizer confirmation; a "visiting relatives" applicant needs proof of relationship). This is a single visa application with a purpose-dependent document checklist, not separate visa products.

**Nuance found — some consulates present per-nationality "business" checklists as their own navigational item**: The corpus's existing japan-visa-china.md file states, per MOFA's China-specific topic page: "Visits for short-term business affairs or visiting relatives/acquaintances require a different visa category ('short-term business affairs' or 'visit to relatives/acquaintances' visa), not a tourist visa, except for the high-income multiple-entry visa category." Similarly, the Consulate-General of Japan in Miami's website navigation lists "Temporary Visitor Visa," "Short-Term Business Visa," and "Transit Visa" as separate menu items. Read together with the Boston page above, the most accurate characterization is: **these are different document-checklist tracks for different stated purposes within the same underlying "Temporary Visitor" status of residence under Japan's Immigration Control Act — not separate immigration statuses.** Business travelers, especially from certain nationalities (e.g., China, per the multiple-entry business-visa eligibility criteria referenced in MOFA bilateral documents), may qualify for streamlined or longer-validity multiple-entry visas tied specifically to the business purpose, but the visa itself remains within the Temporary Visitor / Short-Term Stay framework, capped at 90 days per stay like tourism.

**Practical implication for this corpus**: Do not create or route travelers to a separate "Japan business visa" product. For general (nationality-agnostic) business-trip guidance, this file and uk-standard-visitor-visa.md-style framing apply: business purposes are covered by the same visa/eligibility routing already documented in the tourism-purpose files (japan-visa-exemption-list.md, japan-visa-china.md, japan-visa-india.md). For nationality-specific business document checklists (which do vary, as shown above), point the traveler to the relevant per-nationality MOFA topic page rather than assuming this general file covers checklist specifics for every nationality.
