Source page: "Visa to pass through the UK in transit: Overview," GOV.UK, fetched directly (live page, no archive fallback needed).

**Who might need one**: A transit visa may be required when passing through the UK to another country. Exemptions apply for travelers who hold: an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA), an EU Settlement Scheme family permit, a Home Office travel document, a Standard Visitor visa, or a Marriage Visitor visa. Whether a specific nationality needs a transit visa at all is a separate routing question, covered by GOV.UK's official visa checker tool and not duplicated here.

**Two distinct transit visa types, distinguished by whether the traveler clears UK border control**:

1. **Direct Airside Transit Visa (DATV)**: "Apply for a Direct Airside Transit visa if you'll be changing flights in the UK and will not be going through UK border control." The traveler stays airside in the international transit area and never formally enters the UK.

2. **Visitor in Transit Visa**: "Apply for a Visitor in Transit visa if you'll be going through UK border control but leaving the UK within 48 hours." This applies to travelers who must pass through border control (e.g., to change airports, collect luggage and recheck it, or make a landside connection) but whose sole purpose is to continue their journey onward.

**How to tell which one applies**: The distinction depends on whether the traveler will pass through UK border control on arrival — GOV.UK states the traveler's airline can clarify this.

**Duration / when a different visa is needed instead**: The Visitor in Transit visa permits a stay of under 48 hours. If the traveler needs to stay longer than 48 hours, or needs to pass through the UK frequently over a period of more than six months, GOV.UK states they "need to apply for a Standard Visitor visa instead of a Visitor in Transit visa" (see uk-standard-visitor-visa.md for that visa's requirements).

**Application process** (per GOV.UK's "Visa to pass through the UK in transit: Apply" page, fetched directly, live page): "Apply online for a Direct Airside Transit visa or a Visitor in Transit visa" via the Home Office's online visa application portal. "You'll usually get a decision on your visa within 3 weeks," though faster/priority services may be available depending on location. After applying online, applicants attend an in-person appointment at a visa application centre for biometric data collection (fingerprints and photo).

**Fees**: Neither the overview page nor the application page itemizes the DATV or Visitor in Transit visa fee directly — GOV.UK instead directs applicants to a separate fee-calculator tool (visa-fees.homeoffice.gov.uk) that was not accessible as a static fetchable page in this session, so no fee figure is confirmed here. (For comparison, uk-standard-visitor-visa.md — a different visa product — separately documents a reduced £74.50 Standard Visitor visa fee for applicants for whom transiting is their only reason for coming to the UK; that figure applies to the Standard Visitor route, not to the DATV or Visitor in Transit visa specifically, and should not be conflated with either.)
